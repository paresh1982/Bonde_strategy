# Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee): Risk Management and Position Sizing

## 1. Core Philosophy of Risk Management
Pradeep Bonde emphasizes that trading success is fundamentally about **asymmetric risk-to-reward management**, not prediction. In Stockbee's framework:
- Your win rate does not need to exceed 40% to 50% to achieve triple-digit annual returns.
- What matters is keeping losses small (0.5R to 1R) while capturing outsized winners (3R, 5R, 10R+).
- **Rule Zero**: Never let a single trade cause emotional or financial damage to your portfolio.

---

## 2. The R-Multiple and Account Risk Model

### Definition of 1R
- **1R** is defined as the predetermined dollar amount you are willing to lose if a trade hits your stop-loss.
- In Bonde's system, **1R is strictly limited to 0.5% to 1.0% of total portfolio equity**.
  - Example: On a $100,000 account:
    - 0.5% Risk = $500 max loss (1R = $500).
    - 1.0% Risk = $1,000 max loss (1R = $1,000).

### Position Sizing Formula
Position sizing is mathematically derived from the distance between your entry price and your technical stop-loss:

$$\text{Position Size (Shares)} = \frac{\text{Account Equity} \times \text{Risk Percentage}}{\text{Entry Price} - \text{Stop Loss Price}}$$

#### Practical Example:
- Account Size: $100,000
- Risk per trade (1R): 1.0% = $1,000
- Stock Entry Price: $50.00
- Technical Stop Loss (LOD or Consolidation Low): $48.00
- Risk per share: $50.00 - $48.00 = $2.00 (4% stop)
- **Position Size**: $\frac{\$1,000}{\$2.00} = 500 \text{ shares}$ ($25,000 total capital allocation, or 25% of account).

Notice: If the technical stop requires an 8% distance ($4.00), the share size shrinks to 250 shares ($12,500 capital). The dollar risk remains identically $1,000.

## 3. Stop-Loss Placement Principles

1. **Structural Technical Stops**:
   - Stops must be anchored to structural price levels where the trading thesis is invalidated (Low of Day, Low of Consolidation Shelf, or 10 EMA / 20 EMA).
   - Never use arbitrary percentage stops disconnected from chart structure. If the technical stop is too wide (>5% to 7%), skip the trade or wait for an anticipation setup with a tighter pivot.
2. **The "Never Move Stops Away" Rule**:
   - Once a stop-loss order is placed, it can only be adjusted in the direction of the trade (moved up to trail or protect profit). It must NEVER be lowered or widened.
3. **Time Stops**:
   - If a breakout stock fails to expand within 1 to 3 days and merely drifts sideways or shows heavy churning volume, Bonde advocates closing or cutting the trade before the actual stop is touched.

## 4. Profit-Taking & Trade Management Framework

### Step 1: Taking Quick Profits (3 to 5 Days / +2R to +3R)
- Momentum surges tend to exhaust after 3 to 5 consecutive days of upward expansion.
- **Rule**: Sell **1/3 to 1/2 of your total shares** into the strength of the 3rd to 5th day of momentum or once the trade reaches +2R to +3R profit.
- This secures profits, covers all transactional costs, and guarantees that the trade cannot be a net loss.

### Step 2: Moving the Stop to Breakeven
- Immediately upon booking partial profits, elevate the stop-loss on the remaining shares to the original entry price (Breakeven).
- This creates a **"free trade"** where the remaining shares are risk-free.

### Step 3: Trailing the Balance (Letting Runners Run)
- The remainder of the position (1/2 or 1/3) is held for a larger multi-week or multi-month trend using a trailing mechanic:
  - **8 EMA or 10 EMA Close**: Exit on the close of the day if price finishes below the rising 8 or 10 EMA.
  - **Previous Day's Low**: For hyper-momentum trades, trail a stop below the prior day's low each morning.
  - **Climax Exit**: If the stock goes vertical, gapping up repeatedly and moving >30% above its 10 EMA in a few days, sell the entire position into peak retail euphoria.

## 5. Portfolio Heat and Progressive Exposure

Pradeep Bonde teaches that risk management is not just about individual trades; it is about managing **portfolio heat** in sync with overall market conditions:

| Market Condition | Market Breadth Signal | Position Count | Account Exposure | Risk per Trade |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Aggressive Bullish** | 4% Gainers > 100, Losers < 30 | 5 - 10 Positions | 80% - 100%+ (Margin) | 1.0% |
| **Neutral / Choppy** | Gainers & Losers Balanced | 2 - 4 Pilot Positions | 20% - 40% | 0.5% |
| **Bearish / Hostile** | 4% Losers > 100, Gainers < 30 | 0 - 1 Positions | 0% - 10% (Mostly Cash) | 0.25% - 0% |

### Progressive Exposure Concept
- Do not jump from 100% cash to 100% invested overnight.
- Start with 2 to 3 "pilot positions".
- If the pilots work and show immediate profit (+2R), finance additional risk with the market's money and add more positions.
- If the pilots get stopped out, stop buying immediately and retreat to cash.
